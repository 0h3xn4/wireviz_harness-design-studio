# Using HDS in your organisation

This page is for the people who decide whether Harness Design Studio (HDS) may be used in a company: safety, quality and IT. It says what was written by an AI, what the tool checks itself, what a person must still check, what the tool does not claim, and how to check the packages and the network behaviour yourself. It adds no promise to the legal texts; it quotes them.

## In short

- HDS is a desktop program for Ubuntu 24.04 and newer and Windows 10 and 11 ([`INSTALL.md`](../INSTALL.md)). It works offline and keeps every file on your computer.
- Most of it was written by an AI under the direction of one person, the owner. Nobody else has reviewed it.
- It checks its own results in several ways, and it says what it could not check. It does not replace your review.
- It makes no claim of compliance with any standard.
- You can check the downloads, their signatures and the offline behaviour yourself, with the steps named below.

## What was written by an AI

These are the legal texts of the project, copied word for word from their one source, [`compliance/legal/texts.md`](../../compliance/legal/texts.md):

> Most of the code, tests and documentation were generated by Claude (Anthropic) through Claude Code, under the direction of the owner. The owner set the requirements, selected, directed, reviewed and approved the work.

> No person other than the owner has reviewed the tool, and it makes no claim of compliance with any standard.

> Copyright is claimed only to the extent the applicable law allows; purely AI-generated material may not be protected by copyright in some countries. The GPL-3.0-only licence applies to all parts in which copyright exists.

What this means in practice:

- The program code, the tests and these pages came out of AI sessions. The owner set what to build, reviewed the work and decided what to merge and release. Each change is a pull request with its tests; the decisions are in [`DECISIONS.md`](../DECISIONS.md) and the release records in [`compliance/SIGNOFF.md`](../../compliance/SIGNOFF.md).
- **The program has no AI inside it.** It does not call a model or any other service, and it sends nothing over the network ([evidence below](#evidence-you-can-check-yourself)). The same design gives the same result every time.
- The engineering numbers are not written by the AI either. The tool ships no derating or EMC values and no approved parts list. Until your project supplies them, the results say they rest on placeholders and example parts ([fill in the engineering values](fill-in-engineering-values.md), [see what the tool assumes](see-what-the-tool-assumes.md)).

## What the tool checks itself

| Check | What it does | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The self-check** | Checks the generated harnesses against the design with a second program path that shares no code with the generator. | After every generation, in the editor's **Harness** tab, and with `harness-design-studio verify DIR` ([generate harnesses](generate-harnesses.md#2-check-the-result-on-its-own)). |
| **The output check** | Compares every written table, workbook sheet, drawing text and data file with the design. If it fails, nothing is written. | Before every export; `harness-design-studio verify DIR --outputs` repeats it later ([export the outputs](export-the-outputs.md#7-check-that-the-outputs-are-current)). |
| **The design rules** | 39 rules, each with its reason and its fix ([`RULES.md`](../RULES.md)). A rule that lacks a value says *not checked*, not *passed*. | In the editor a moment after each change; `harness-design-studio drc DIR` on the command line ([how auto-linking and the checks work](generate-harnesses.md#how-auto-linking-and-the-checks-work)). |
| **The tests in CI** | On every change: the test suite with a coverage limit of 90% on the core, the lint and type checks (`ruff`, `mypy`), the licence check (`reuse lint`), the test that the program uses no network, the build of the Linux packages and their self-test, and the tests on Windows. | On every pull request and on `master` (the workflows are in `.github/workflows/`). |
| **The end-to-end test before a release** | A test of the packages of a release before its tag: install, a walk through the commands, the editor under a virtual display, the upgrade of an older project, the offline run. | Before the tags of 1.17.1 and 2.0.0; what passed and what did not is written in [`compliance/SIGNOFF.md`](../../compliance/SIGNOFF.md#publication-of-200-2026-11-15). |

These checks are made by the same project that made the tool. They find faults of the kinds they look for. They do not show that the tool has no faults, and they do not stand in for an independent review.

## What you must verify

The intended use, word for word:

> Harness Design Studio supports harness design work. Every harness design must be checked by qualified engineers under your own processes before it is built or flown.

> Provided as is, without warranty of any kind. Check all results before you rely on them. See sections 15 and 16 of the GPL-3.0.

What a person checks, at least:

- **Every value the tool did not decide.** Derating, wire current ratings, EMC classes, test limits and the approved parts come from your project. The notes of `drc` list what was not checked because a value is missing ([read and fix problems](read-and-fix-problems.md#what-the-notes-mean)).
- **Every choice the tool made for you.** Connectors marked *auto-filled* stay marked until a person confirms them. Pins given by the allocator follow a rule you can read with `explain` ([pin allocation](../models/pin-allocation.md)).
- **The pinouts that came in.** A KiCad or spreadsheet import is only as right as its source; read the printed rows ([import KiCad pinouts](import-kicad-pinouts.md)).
- **The written outputs** that people build from: the build workbook, the wire list, the labels and the test tables ([export the outputs](export-the-outputs.md)). They carry the notice *Provided as is, without warranty. Verify before use.*
- **Every waiver.** A waived warning stays in the report with its reason; a reviewer reads the reasons.

The tool records who released a harness and why, but it cannot prove who typed a name. Use your own review and sign-off process for that.

## No claim of compliance

HDS makes no claim of compliance with any standard. Some design rules cite a requirement of an ECSS or ESCC standard by its ID, to say where the rule comes from; that is not a statement that a design or the tool meets it. The text of those standards is not in the repository. The waivers the owner gave for the process requirements of this project (independent review, usability sessions and others) are listed in [`compliance/DEVIATIONS.md`](../../compliance/DEVIATIONS.md); a waiver accepts a gap and does not close it.

## Evidence you can check yourself

- **Offline, no telemetry.** The app collects no personal data and sends nothing over the network. All files stay on your computer. A name is written into a file only when you type it (as the author of a change, a run or a release); the app looks up no user name and no computer name. A colleague can check this in about 30 minutes, without network, on the package itself: [`VERIFY_OFFLINE.md`](../VERIFY_OFFLINE.md). The same audit runs on every release, and its report is attached to the release page. The one exception is a Python plug-in that a project enables and you trust; your IT department can switch plug-ins off ([switch plug-ins off](../VERIFY_OFFLINE.md#switch-python-plug-ins-off-on-your-computers)).
- **Signatures and provenance.** The releases after 1.17.0 are signed with Sigstore: the release workflow signs the packages, without a key, and adds a build provenance attestation. That shows which workflow of which repository made a file; the Windows packages have no code-signing certificate, so Windows shows a warning on the first start. How to check: [`INSTALL.md`, *Check the signature*](../INSTALL.md#check-the-signature); what it means and what it does not: [`SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md). The owner decided to skip the check of the signatures from outside the workflow for the published releases, so nobody outside the workflow has done it yet ([`compliance/SIGNOFF.md`](../../compliance/SIGNOFF.md)).
- **What is in the package.** The release pages of the signed releases carry the software bill of materials (SBOM), the inventory of the files, the licence notices and the sources of the LGPL and GPL parts. The security review of the program is in [`SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md).
- **Where it runs.** Ubuntu 24.04 and newer, and Windows 10 and 11 (a setup program that installs for your user only, or a portable `.zip`; neither needs an administrator account, [`INSTALL.md`, Windows](../INSTALL.md#d-windows-from-120)). The Windows packages were built and tested on GitHub's Windows runners; nobody has tried them on a real company computer.

## Questions for your own process

These are questions for your organisation; the tool does not answer them for you.

1. May a program written mostly by an AI and reviewed by one person be used for design work here, and at which step: drafts, block diagrams, harness plans, build documents?
2. Who checks the results, and how is that recorded (your own review and sign-off, not the tool's release record alone)?
3. Which values (derating, ratings, EMC classes, test limits, approved parts) does the project supply, and who owns them?
4. May the program be installed on company computers? The checks of [`VERIFY_OFFLINE.md`](../VERIFY_OFFLINE.md) and the signature check are the evidence IT usually asks for.
5. Should Python plug-ins be switched off on your computers?
